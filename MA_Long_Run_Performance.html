Does M&A create value?Long-run performance of US acquirers, 2012–2021

Announcement-day returns tell you what the market expects. This page follows 786 significant acquisitions by US listed companies for three to five years after closing, and measures what actually happened to the acquirer's shareholders, margins and goodwill.

The question

A board is weighing a large acquisition. Across a decade of US deals, did acquirers beat comparable stocks after closing, and which kinds of deal did better or worse?

Is it the benchmark?

Long-horizon returns are fragile: small errors in the benchmark compound over three years, and a few extreme deals move the average. The result is tested three independent ways.

Calendar-time portfolio alphas

Every month, all acquirers within 36 months of closing; alpha is the return not explained by the factors (Newey-West t-statistics).

Robustness of the 36-month result

Same measure, different choices. The t-statistic is skewness-adjusted.

Method

  1. Deals. Every US filer's acquisition spending, fiscal years 2011 to 2022, from SEC XBRL frames: cash paid for acquisitions, stock issued for acquisitions and consideration transferred. Kept when the deal value is at least $100m, the acquirer filed an 8-K Item 2.01 (completion of an acquisition) that year, and the value is at least 10% of the acquirer's market value. Banks, insurers and utilities are excluded; one deal per acquirer every three years.
  2. Completion date. The 8-K Item 2.01 filing date. Month 1 is the first full month after it.
  3. Prices. Month-end closes from DoltHub, including delisted stocks, adjusted for splits and dividends. Each SEC filer is matched to its ticker and every match is checked: price times shares outstanding must be consistent with the public float on the 10-K cover. Splits missing from the price data, splits recorded a month early and ticker changes are corrected when the company's own share count confirms them.
  4. Benchmark. Each acquirer is assigned at completion to one of the 25 Fama-French portfolios formed on size and book-to-market, using NYSE breakpoints.
  5. BHAR. BHARi,H = ∏(1 + ri,t) − ∏(1 + rb,t), t = 1..H If the acquirer stops trading, the rest of the window earns the benchmark.
  6. Inference. Skewness-adjusted t-statistic with a bootstrap p-value (Lyon, Barber and Tsai, 1999), Wilcoxon test on the median, and calendar-time portfolio regressions on the Fama-French five factors plus momentum.
  7. Operating performance. Operating income / average assets minus the two-digit SIC industry median from all SEC filers; post-deal (average of years +1 to +3) regressed on pre-deal (year −1), following Healy, Palepu and Ruback (1992).
  8. Point in time. Prices, returns, filings and accounting periods end on 31 March 2025; cover-page data are used only when public by then. Five-year results use deals completed by March 2020.
